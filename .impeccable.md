## Design Context

### Users
Solo, technically fluent user (the developer), on desktop Linux and Android. The
screen gets opened for one of two reasons: to throw the chain on or off, or to
answer "is my traffic actually going where I think it is, right now?" — usually
in a few seconds, often at a desk at night. No onboarding, no explaining what an
entry hop is. Assume total fluency with the chain's own vocabulary.

### Brand Personality
Machined · live · candid.

A piece of equipment, not an app. It should feel like a dedicated device
manufactured for this one job — something with a front panel, a weight to it,
and a switch you throw. Confidence comes from the object feeling real and the
readouts being demonstrably true, never from decoration or reassurance. When
protection is absent, the panel says so plainly, and says why.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Tactile hardware — a front-panel instrument.**

The window is an enclosure, not a page: a modeled panel with bezels, inset
wells, and seams, lit consistently from the top-left so highlights sit on upper
edges and shadow falls below. Depth is physical (machined relief), never
Material elevation. Dark-first — a dark anodized enclosure is the designed
surface; light theme is the same object in a lighter alloy, a faithful
translation rather than a second design.

Signature elements:
- **Lamps, not badges.** State is an indicator lamp: hot core, soft bloom
  spilling onto the surrounding panel, dead dark well when off. Lamp colors come
  from real LED phosphors — amber, oxide red, signal green — not an arbitrary
  brand ramp.
- **A switch, not a button.** Connect/disconnect is a throw with travel,
  resistance, and a settle at the end of its motion.
- **Engraved labels.** Panel text reads as cut into the surface: small,
  uppercase, tracked out, with a hairline light/dark edge. Never glowing text.
- **The route map is a screen set into the panel** — its own recessed well with
  its own backlight tone, distinct from the enclosure material.
- **Always alive.** While connected, something is genuinely in motion: packets
  traversing the route, counters ticking, lamps reacting to traffic. Motion is
  weighty and mechanical — things settle, nothing bounces — and always
  represents measured state, never idle decoration.

**Borrow from ProtonVPN** (structure only, never its palette): the map as the
centerpiece rather than an illustration beside the controls; Secure Core's habit
of *drawing the multi-hop path* across it instead of naming endpoints in a list;
one unmistakable connection control that dominates the composition; a details
readout that states protocol, exit IP, and protection status without
interpretation. Explicitly do **not** take Proton's violet brand color, its soft
rounded card surfaces, or its consumer-reassurance tone.

Anti-references — the design fails if it drifts toward any of these:
- Hacker/crypto neon: Matrix green, cyan glow on black, terminal cosplay,
  scanlines as decoration.
- Default Material 3: stock components, rounded cards with drop shadows,
  dynamic-color purple.
- Apple-style frosted minimal: glassmorphism, heavy blur, translucency,
  oversized corner radii, whitespace as the whole idea.

Typography should read as manufactured rather than authored: an industrial
grotesk for panel labels, paired with a wide mechanical mono for numeric
readouts (IPs, RTT, counters). Move off the current Barlow / Fragment Mono
pairing — too neutral to feel like an object — and avoid the ubiquitous
technical defaults (Inter, IBM Plex, Space Mono, DM, Jakarta).

### Design Principles
1. **The panel is an object.** Every surface belongs to one modeled enclosure
   with one light source. If an element couldn't be manufactured, it doesn't
   belong on the panel.
2. **Readouts are measured, never assumed.** Anything presented as fact comes
   from something observed at runtime. Configured values are labeled as
   configured; unknown is shown as unknown.
3. **Never overstate protection.** When the chain isn't fail-closed, say so, say
   the specific reason, and say the specific remedy.
4. **Motion means telemetry.** Animation is reserved for representing live
   state. A still panel means nothing is happening.
5. **Hierarchy is machined, not colored.** Size, weight, engraving, and panel
   grouping carry hierarchy. Accent is rare and semantic — it's a lamp, and
   lamps are expensive.
